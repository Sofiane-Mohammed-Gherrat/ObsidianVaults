## Volume Absorption – Skincare Oil

Skincare oils (rosehip, argan, jojoba, squalane, etc.) are typically **more refined and translucent** than cooking oils, with a lighter, more luxurious appearance.

### Recommended Values

|Oil Type|Density|Color|
|---|---|---|
|Squalane / Jojoba|**0.1 – 0.4**|Almost clear, faint yellow (0.95, 0.90, 0.75)|
|Argan oil|**0.3 – 0.8**|Warm golden (0.85, 0.70, 0.35)|
|Rosehip oil|**0.5 – 1.2**|Amber-orange (0.80, 0.45, 0.20)|
|Sea buckthorn blend|**1.0 – 2.0**|Deep orange (0.75, 0.30, 0.05)|

---

### Key Differences from Cooking Oil

- **Lower density overall** — skincare oils are filtered and refined, so light passes through more cleanly
- **Warmer, more golden tones** — less green, more amber/honey, which reads as premium on renders
- **Subtle scatter** — add Volume Scatter at **0.02 – 0.08** to give that slight luminous glow from within, very characteristic of high-end serum/oil product shots
- **Higher IOR** — use **1.47 – 1.48** on the fluid mesh for accuracy

---

### For a Luxury Commercial Look

Since this aligns with your high-end product visualization style, a few extra notes:

- Keep absorption **on the lighter end** of the range — thinner oil reads more premium
- A faint **warm golden color** with low density looks expensive under studio lighting
- Backlit setups will make the volume absorption glow beautifully — worth compositing a separate **volume light pass**
- Pair with a clean, slightly green-tinted glass (density ~0.05) for contrast against the warm oil
