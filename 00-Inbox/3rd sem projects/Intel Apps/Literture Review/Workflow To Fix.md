**Why part 4 first:**

- Writing yours well sets a quality bar that might naturally pressure the others to improve theirs.

**The disconnection risk is real but manageable.** The main seam to watch is between your section and the conclusion (Section 5) — if your part surfaces challenges that the conclusion doesn't address, it'll feel orphaned. 1. Finish and polish Part 4 first.

# Simple Literature Review Workflow

## Step 1: Define a narrow question

Don't start with a broad topic.

❌ Bad:

> Artificial Intelligence in Education

✅ Better:

> The impact of AI chatbots on university students' learning outcomes

Everything you read should help answer that question. ([Department of Psychology](https://psychology.ucsd.edu/undergraduate-program/undergraduate-resources/academic-writing-resources/writing-research-papers/writing-lit-review.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Writing a Literature Review"))

---

## Step 2: Collect 10–20 Relevant Sources

For a typical undergraduate project:

- Journal articles
- Conference papers
- Review papers
- Recent studies (last 5 years preferred)

Create a table like:

|Paper|Year|Method|Main Findings|Limitations|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|Smith (2024)|2024|Survey|AI improved engagement|Small sample|
|Tan (2023)|2023|Experiment|Better grades observed|Short duration|

Do this for every paper.

### Nuance

Don't read every paper fully.

Read in this order:

1. Abstract
2. Conclusion
3. Results
4. Introduction
5. Methodology (only if needed)

This can cut reading time by more than half.

---

## Step 3: Extract Themes

This is where most students fail.

A literature review is **not**:

> Paper A says...
> 
> Paper B says...
> 
> Paper C says...

That's just a summary.

Instead identify themes.

Example:

Topic:

> AI Chatbots in Education

After reading 15 papers you might notice:

### Theme 1: Learning Performance

- 7 studies found grade improvements
- 2 studies found no significant difference

### Theme 2: Student Engagement

- Most studies reported increased participation
- Some reported dependency concerns

### Theme 3: Ethical Concerns

- Privacy
- Academic integrity
- Bias

Now you have the structure of your literature review. ([Scribbr](https://www.scribbr.com/dissertation/literature-review/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "How to Write a Literature Review | Guide, Examples, & Templates"))

## Step 4: Build a Synthesis Matrix

This is the most useful technique.

|Author|Performance|Engagement|Ethics|
|---|---|---|---|
|Smith|✓|✓||
|Tan|✓||✓|
|Lee||✓|✓|

Now patterns become obvious.

You start seeing:

- agreements
- disagreements
- gaps

This is where actual academic analysis begins.

## Step 5: Find Gaps and Contradictions

Lecturers love this.

Ask:

### What do studies agree on?

Example:

> Most studies agree that AI chatbots increase engagement.

### What do they disagree on?

Example:

> Some studies report grade improvement while others find no statistically significant impact.

### What is missing?

Example:

> Most studies focus on Western universities, with limited evidence from Southeast Asian institutions.

These become your critical analysis paragraphs. ([Scribbr](https://www.scribbr.com/dissertation/literature-review/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "How to Write a Literature Review | Guide, Examples, & Templates"))

## 🛠️ Part 2: Workflow to Replicate This Organization

To replicate this sophisticated structure, you need to transition from simply summarizing articles (a traditional review) to **structuring knowledge** (a systematic review).

Follow these 7 phases sequentially:

### Phase 1: Defining the Scope and Problem (The "Why")

*   **Action:** Start by defining your central concept (e.g., "Sustainable Architecture," "Machine Learning in Healthcare").
*   **Deliverable:** The **Knowledge Gap**. Do not just say, "Few people have studied X." Say, "While research exists on A and B, there is no single systematic review that classifies the fusion of A and B within a specific context like C (your domain)."
*   **Goal:** To make your entire review sound *necessary*.

### Phase 2: Setting the Scientific Rules (The Methodology)

This section must be rigid. Treat it like an experiment protocol.
1.  **Select Protocol:** Adopt or mention a recognized standard (e.g., PRISMA).
2.  **Define Boundaries:** Explicitly state your search years, databases (IEEE, PubMed, Scopus), and keywords used.
3.  **Detail Filtering:** Document the filtering process: *How many* initial articles were retrieved? How many were removed at each stage (relevance check $\to$ abstract check $\to$ full-text review)? This proves your rigor.

### Phase 3: Structuring the Results by Theme, Not Alphabetically

This is the most critical structural shift. **Do not organize the results by author or year.** Organize them by *theme* or *domain*.

*   **If studying Technology:** Group findings by *Application Area* (e.g., "Industrial Robotics," "Agricultural Monitoring").

*   **If studying Science:** Group findings by *Process Step* (e.g., "Detection Phase," "Modeling Phase," "Prediction Phase").

*   **Within each Domain/Theme Section, create sub-sections for:**
	
    1.  The **Problem** the fusion solves.
    2.  The **Inputs** (What data sources are combined: e.g., Camera + LiDAR).
    3.  The **Techniques/Algorithms** used to combine them (e.g., Kalman Filter, Deep Learning CNNs).

### Phase 4: Creating the Synthesis Tools (Summary & Taxonomy)

Do not just end after describing the domains. You must synthesize the knowledge using tools like tables.
*   **Goal:** To provide a comprehensive "map" of the field.
*   **Create a Master Table:** A table that lists:

| Domain   | Specific Application | Input Data Sources (Inputs) | Fusion Technique Used | Key Outcome/Limitation              |
| :------- | :------------------- | :-------------------------- | :-------------------- | :---------------------------------- |
| Military | Target Tracking      | RF Signals, GPS, Camera     | Kalman Filter         | Improved accuracy in rough terrain. |

### Phase 5: Conclusion and Future Scope (The "What's Next")
1.  **Reiterate Contribution:** State clearly what your review achieved ("We successfully classified the use of X...").
2.  **List Limitations:** Be modest. Acknowledge what the study *did not* cover (e.g., computational complexity, long-term feasibility).
3.  **Propose Future Work:** Based on your limitations, suggest 3–5 specific areas that future researchers should investigate. This shows mastery of the topic and keeps your paper relevant for citation.
