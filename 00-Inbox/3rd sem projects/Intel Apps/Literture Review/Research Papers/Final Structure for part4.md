
# Introduction:

> A Part 4 introduction typically does four things, each in a sentence or two: frames the problem, states the scope, previews the structure, and connects to the rest of the review.

The framing opens with why caregiver access to trustworthy information matters at all — something like establishing that even where ASD knowledge exists in principle, caregivers face real barriers in finding, trusting, and acting on it, which directly affects outcomes for the child and family. This doesn't need citations yet; it's orienting the reader before the evidence comes in.

The scope statement is where you explicitly name the four areas your brief covers — language/literacy barriers, trust in online health information, emotional state during information-seeking, and stigma — so the reader knows upfront what this section will and won't address. This is also a good place to be honest that one of these (language/literacy) is treated as an identified gap rather than a reviewed area, since Theme 4 frames it that way; flagging it in the intro means it doesn't feel like an omission later.

The structural preview is a short roadmap sentence: something like "this section is organized into five thematic areas, beginning with caregivers' baseline knowledge and information access, followed by the role of trust in digital health information, the influence of stigma and emotional burden, structural barriers that shape information-seeking behavior, and finally the implications of these findings for AutiGuide's design." This maps directly onto your Theme 1–5 files (Theme 6 is usually better left out of the intro's roadmap, since it's the synthesis/conclusion rather than a "theme" being reviewed — it gets its own framing at the point it appears).

## 3. How to Cleanly Balance Your Introduction

If you feel like your introduction is becoming "cluttered" with too many parenthetical citations, you can switch to **Narrative Citations** to make the prose flow better.

Instead of putting everything in brackets at the end of the sentence, bring the authors into the story:

> "While global research highlights systemic failures in low-to-middle-income countries, **Chu et al. (2020)** demonstrate that these challenges are particularly acute for Malaysian parents navigating local intervention pathways."
