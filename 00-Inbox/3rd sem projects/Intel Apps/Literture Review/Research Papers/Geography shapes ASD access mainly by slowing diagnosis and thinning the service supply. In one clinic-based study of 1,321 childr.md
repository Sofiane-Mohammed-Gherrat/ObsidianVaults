Geography shapes ASD access mainly by slowing diagnosis and thinning the service supply. In one clinic-based study of 1,321 children in India, rural children were diagnosed about 0.35 years later than urban children. [^1] A U.S. access study found that younger age at diagnosis clustered in areas with more neurologists and psychiatrists and proximity to a medical school, while primary-care density and proximity to early-intervention agencies did not show the same signal. [^2]

For intervention services, the pattern is similar: rural families report longer travel, more time spent reaching care, and more unmet need for behavior support and parent support groups; one study also found board-certified behavior analysts were present in a lower percentage of rural counties. [^3] A Frontiers review ties those gaps to distance, specialist scarcity, low reliance on health professionals, and cultural barriers, and it points to telehealth as a partial workaround rather than a full solution. [^4] The picture is not perfectly uniform, though: one small study found the apparent geographic difference in diagnosis largely disappeared after adjusting for socioeconomic and medical factors, and another report actually found better satisfaction and a lower age of diagnosis in more rural areas. [^5]

So the evidence I found says geographic disparities usually mean later diagnosis, fewer local specialists, and more travel burden, but some of the apparent rural–urban gap is entangled with socioeconomic status, provider mix, and how services are organized. This is an initial search, so a more targeted pass could surface more region-specific studies or evaluations of telehealth and navigation programs.

[^1]: Prahbhjot & Singhi, 2022. Age at diagnosis for autism spectrum disorders: Does it differ by place of residence?. Indian Journal of Public Health.

[^2]: Kalkbrenner et al., 2011. Geographic Access to Health Services and Diagnosis with an Autism Spectrum Disorder. Annals of Epidemiology.

[^3]: Mello et al., 2016. Services for Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder: Comparing Rural and Non-Rural Communities. Education and Training in Autism and Developmental Disabilities.

[^4]: Antezana et al., 2017. Rural Trends in Diagnosis and Services for Autism Spectrum Disorder. Frontiers in Psychology.

[^5]: Ghahari et al., 2022. Rural-Urban Differences in Age at Autism Diagnosis: A Multiple Model Analysis. Iranian Journal of Psychiatry.
