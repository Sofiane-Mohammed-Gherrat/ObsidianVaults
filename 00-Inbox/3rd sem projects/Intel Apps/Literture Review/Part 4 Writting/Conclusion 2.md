### 4.3 Conclusion and Rationale for AutiGuide

Taken together, the literature suggests that caregivers of children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) face a combination of informational, social, and structural challenges that rarely occur in isolation. Many parents begin their caregiving journey with limited ASD-related knowledge and may struggle to understand diagnoses, navigate intervention pathways, and identify reliable sources of support (Chu et al., 2020). These difficulties are further complicated by an information environment in which credible resources coexist alongside misinformation and unsupported health claims (Dobrogowska-Schlebusch, 2016). Access to and effective use of information also appears to be influenced by educational background, socioeconomic status, and other demographic factors (Mohd Hussain et al., 2022).

At the same time, caregivers often manage these information-related challenges while experiencing the broader psychological and social pressures associated with ASD caregiving. Stigma, reduced social participation, and the emotional demands of caring for a child with ASD can affect overall family well-being and add further complexity to the search for guidance and support (Papadopoulos et al., 2019; Chu et al., 2020). These challenges do not exist independently of the wider healthcare environment. Structural factors such as unequal access to specialist services and the financial burden associated with long-term care may limit the support available to many families and influence how they seek information and assistance (Kalkbrenner et al., 2011; Ou et al., 2015).

Viewed collectively, these findings suggest that existing sources of support often leave caregivers navigating fragmented information, uneven service access, and considerable practical constraints. While online resources are widely used, their quality and reliability cannot always be assumed (Twombly et al., 2011; Dobrogowska-Schlebusch, 2016). At the same time, formal support services may be constrained by geographic and financial factors (Kalkbrenner et al., 2011; Ou et al., 2015). This creates a need for accessible, trustworthy sources of guidance that can complement existing services and help caregivers make informed decisions.

Within this context, the proposed AutiGuide framework offers a potential response to several of the challenges identified in the literature. By using a curated knowledge base of evidence-based ASD information and a retrieval-based architecture, AutiGuide is designed to provide caregivers with consistent and trustworthy guidance through a digital platform. Its low-cost and accessible nature may help reduce some of the barriers associated with geography, service availability, and information fragmentation, while providing caregivers with a single point of access to verified ASD-related resources.

Nevertheless, important gaps remain within the literature. None of the reviewed studies directly examine how language proficiency, health literacy, or a caregiver's emotional state influence the process of seeking and interpreting ASD-related information. These areas remain underexplored and may represent important directions for future research. Evaluating how tools such as AutiGuide perform across different literacy levels, linguistic backgrounds, and caregiving circumstances may provide valuable insights into how digital support systems can better serve families in the future.
