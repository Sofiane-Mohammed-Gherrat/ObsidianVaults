# 4.3 Synthesis of Challenges and Rationale for AutiGuide:

The challenges facing ASD caregivers in Malaysia are not isolated difficulties — they form an interconnected chain of failures, each one reinforcing the next. At the root lies a systemic reality: geographic distance from specialized centers, prohibitive therapy costs, and institutional bottlenecks deny many families timely access to formal support, forcing them into prolonged uncertainty before a diagnosis is even possible (Kalkbrenner et al., 2011; Ou et al., 2015). This infrastructural void is not merely an inconvenience — it is the primary driver that sets the remaining challenges in motion.

Deprived of reliable formal channels, caregivers are compelled to turn to digital platforms — not by preference, but by necessity. Yet this environment offers no safe ground. Parents enter online spaces with limited baseline knowledge of autism, leaving them poorly equipped to evaluate what they encounter (Chu et al., 2020). The same emotional need for community that draws caregivers to digital spaces also lowers their critical guard, exposing them to misinformation, predatory health claims, and pseudo-scientific narratives that spread rapidly and unchecked (Dobrogowska-Schlebusch, 2016; Twombly et al., 2011). Even caregivers who resist acting on unverified content are not immune — passive exposure gradually erodes trust in medical professionals, quietly reshaping how parents relate to clinical guidance over time (Mohd Hussain et al., 2022).

Compounding this further is the psychological toll that runs beneath every search and every decision. Social stigma, financial instability, and diagnostic uncertainty generate a state of chronic stress and emotional exhaustion that fundamentally alters how caregivers process information (Papadopoulos et al., 2019; Chu et al., 2020). A parent navigating acute burnout does not evaluate sources with the same critical distance as one operating from a place of stability. The emotional climate of the household is not a background variable — it is an active determinant of informational outcomes.

What emerges from this synthesis is not simply a shortage of information, but a failure to provide accessible, trustworthy, and context-aware support that meets caregivers where they are — emotionally, linguistically, and structurally. Static websites and generic portals cannot respond to the variability of a caregiver's state. What is required is an intelligent system capable of delivering verified, evidence-based guidance in real time, compensating for knowledge gaps, reducing cognitive load, and providing a private, non-judgmental channel for sensitive questions.

It is within this gap that AutiGuide is positioned. By combining a curated, practitioner-reviewed knowledge base with AI-driven natural language processing and a human-in-the-loop query reformulation layer, AutiGuide is designed not merely to deliver information, but to make trustworthy guidance genuinely accessible to caregivers when institutional support is unavailable, unaffordable, or simply too far away. The literature does not point toward a need for more content — it points toward a need for a smarter, more responsive kind of support. AutiGuide is a direct response to that need.
