
Collectively, Chu et al. (2020), Dobrogowska-Schlebusch (2016), and Mohd Hussain et al. (2022) demonstrate that caregiver information hurdles begin before an active search takes place. Caregivers typically initiate their journey with deficient baseline knowledge, navigate environments prone to misinformation, and face asymmetric information access driven by educational and socioeconomic disparities. Consequently, information barriers stem less from a lack of raw availability and more from systemic constraints on a caregiver's capacity to locate, comprehend, and critically evaluate the resources they encounter.

While obtaining information is challenging, evaluating its trustworthiness creates an additional hurdle as caregivers increasingly pivot to digital platforms. Dobrogowska-Schlebusch (2016) highlights that restricted access to scientific data increases caregiver vulnerability to misinformation and false health claims, yet online information-seeking is nearly universal and acts as a vital channel for both technical facts and social support (Twombly et al., 2011). Consequently, caregivers are routinely exposed to an unverified mixture of evidence-based and unreliable claims. Because the utility of digital platforms depends strictly on the reliability of the resources retrieved (Mohd Hussain et al., 2022), expanding raw availability is insufficient. Caregiver support frameworks must integrate built-in mechanisms to help families actively filter, evaluate, and rely upon trustworthy data within their existing digital ecosystems.

Beyond practical access, caregiving features profound psychological and social challenges. Papadopoulos et al. (2019) demonstrate that autism-related stigma severely undermines caregiver well-being, though personal factors like self-esteem, confidence, and robust social support can moderate these negative effects. Concurrently, the pervasive demands of caregiving disrupt broader family functioning, household well-being, and daily social participation (Chu et al., 2020). Together, these findings indicate that families routinely navigate systemic barriers under intense emotional strain and social pressure. While neither study explicitly links emotional states to real-time information-seeking behaviors, these psychological dimensions provide critical context for how support systems are perceived and utilized by families under stress (Chu et al., 2020; Papadopoulos et al., 2019).

Despite valuable insights into universal barriers, a critical literature gap remains regarding how language proficiency and real-time emotional states actively shape the caregiving information-seeking process. Chu et al. (2020) isolate challenges in navigating intervention pathways, while Papadopoulos et al. (2019) document the heavy psychological burdens of stigma. However, neither study explores if acute emotional factors—such as stress or anxiety—disrupt how caregivers search for and interpret information. Similarly, while Dobrogowska-Schlebusch (2016) and Mohd Hussain et al. (2022) establish baseline data-acquisition metrics, current research overlooks how language barriers or literacy differences filter comprehension. These systemic omissions underscore an underexplored dimension in ASD caregiving research (Chu et al., 2020; Dobrogowska-Schlebusch, 2016; Mohd Hussain et al., 2022; Papadopoulos et al., 2019), directly justifying the development of an emotionally-aware, linguistically simplified support framework like AutiGuide.

Beyond informational and psychological barriers, macro-structural factors heavily dictate the caregiving trajectory. Kalkbrenner et al. (2011) demonstrate that unequal access to specialist services directly determines diagnostic timing, proving that systemic healthcare bottlenecks delay early identification and intervention. Compounding this, maintaining consistent care imposes severe financial burdens, precipitating household income loss and parental employment disruptions (Ou et al., 2015). Together, these findings reveal that caregiving experiences are bound by external geographical and economic constraints. While neither study directly evaluates information-seeking patterns, they provide vital context for why families, facing restricted or delayed access to formal institutional services, increasingly rely on self-directed digital support systems (Kalkbrenner et al., 2011; Ou et al., 2015).

The reviewed literature collectively reveals an interconnected web of systemic caregiver challenges. Families initiate their caregiving journey with limited baseline knowledge, facing profound difficulties understanding diagnoses and navigating intervention pathways (Chu et al., 2020). This deficit is compounded by severe informational barriers, where digital misinformation risks (Dobrogowska-Schlebusch, 2016) and structural inequalities in information-seeking literacy (Mohd Hussain et al., 2022) disrupt data acquisition. Because online platforms are nearly universal yet highly unverified (Dobrogowska-Schlebusch, 2016; Twombly et al., 2011), caregivers navigate these unverified spaces under immense psychological distress, household strain, and societal stigma (Chu et al., 2020; Papadopoulos et al., 2019). Furthermore, macro-structural barriers regarding unequal specialist distribution (Kalkbrenner et al., 2011) and severe financial strain (Ou et al., 2015) continually obstruct formal institutional care. Synthetically, these multi-staged barriers establish an urgent, empirical need for a decentralized, reliable, and accessible support engine—directly justifying the development of the proposed AutiGuide digital framework.
