### Methodology

Islamic commercial law (fiqh al-muamalat) evaluates contracts on several principles:

- **No riba** (interest)
- **No gharar** (excessive uncertainty or ambiguity)
- **No zulm** (oppression or unfair one-sided terms)
- **No qimar** (gambling-like clauses)
- **Mutual consent and transparency**
- **Fulfillment of obligations (amanah)**

---

## ✅ Shariah Compliant Clauses

**Clauses 1 & 2 — Property Inspection & As-Is Possession** Tenant inspects and accepts the property knowingly. This satisfies the Islamic requirement of **full disclosure and informed consent** before entering a contract.

**Clause 3 — Deposits (General Structure)** Holding a security deposit as a guarantee against damage is permissible in Islam. This resembles **rahn** (pledge/collateral) in Islamic finance — a legitimate mechanism to secure obligations.

**Clause 3.3 — Deposit Refund Within 14 Days** Returning what belongs to the tenant promptly is an **amanah** obligation. The 14-day refund window is fair and Shariah compliant.

**Clause 3.4 — Deposits Cannot Be Used as Rent** Keeping deposit and rent obligations separate is clean and transparent. Compliant.

**Clause 4(a) — Pay Rent on 1st of Month** Clear, defined payment obligation on a known date. Satisfies the Islamic requirement of **clarity in financial obligations**.

**Clause 4(c) — Tenant Pays Utilities** Paying for what you consume directly is entirely compliant — no ambiguity, no interest, no unfairness.

**Clause 4(g) — Lawful Purposes Only** Using property only for halal purposes is an explicitly Islamic principle. Fully compliant.

**Clause 4(h) — Avoid Nuisance to Neighbours** Respecting neighbours is a strong Islamic obligation. The Prophet ﷺ repeatedly emphasized the rights of neighbours. Compliant.

**Clause 4(k) — Residential Use Only** Restricting use to residential purposes prevents the property from being used for haram activities. Compliant.

**Clause 4(n) — Return Property in Good Condition** The Islamic principle of **la darar wa la dirar** (no harm shall be inflicted or reciprocated) supports the obligation to return property without causing damage. Compliant.

**Clause 4(r) — Maintain Property in Good Repair** Taking care of what has been entrusted to you is a core **amanah** principle in Islam. Compliant.

**Clause 4(t) — No Dangerous Materials** Prohibiting weapons, explosives, and radioactive substances aligns with Islam's prohibition on facilitating harm. Compliant.

**Clause 5(i) — Landlord Maintains Structure** The landlord maintaining what he owns and has rented out is an obligation recognized in Islamic jurisprudence — a landlord must ensure the property remains fit for use. Compliant.

**Clause 5(ii) — Landlord Pays Quit Rent and Assessment** Owner bearing the costs of ownership is consistent with Islamic property law. Compliant.

**Clause 5(iii) — Quiet Enjoyment** Guaranteeing the tenant undisturbed use of what they have paid for is a basic contractual obligation in Islam — you must deliver what was promised. Compliant.

**Clause 8 — Fire Destruction** Suspending rent when the property becomes uninhabitable is just and fair. Islamic jurisprudence holds that **rent is tied to the usufruct (manfaah)** — if the benefit ceases, so should the payment. Compliant and actually very aligned with Islamic principles.

**Clause 9 — Renewal Option** Offering a renewal by mutual agreement with renegotiated rent is clean and transparent. Compliant.

**Clause 11 — Agreement Supersedes Previous Promises** Clarity and written record of obligations is encouraged in Islam — the Quran explicitly instructs to write down financial dealings (2:282). Compliant.

**Clause 13 — Governed by Malaysian Law** Malaysian law for civil matters is acceptable for Muslims in Malaysia as a governing framework for contracts. Compliant.

**Clause 17 — Tenant Pays Stamp Duty** A known, fixed government charge agreed upon by both parties. Compliant.

## ⚠️ Questionable / Needs Review

**Clause 3.1.1 & 3.1.3 — Landlord Deducts Damage from Deposit** The concept is permissible but the mechanism has **gharar** risk — "damage" is not defined, leaving the deduction amount uncertain and subject to landlord's unilateral assessment. An Islamic contract should have clearer damage assessment procedures to remove ambiguity.

**Clause 3.2.1 — Utility Deposit Used for Unpaid Bills** Permissible in principle but the phrase "outstanding for more than 2 months" introduces some ambiguity. Should specify exactly which bills and what constitutes "outstanding." Minor gharar concern.

**Clause 4(s) — Modifications Require Landlord Permission** The clause itself is fine but it references the **Deed of Mutual Covenants** — an external document not attached to the agreement. Binding a party to unseen terms is a **gharar** concern in Islamic contracts. You should request a copy of the Deed before signing.

**Clause 6 — Landlord's Right to Terminate and Forfeit** The termination right is legitimate but the **automatic forfeiture of both deposits** as damages is questionable. In Islamic finance, penalties must be proportional to actual harm suffered — blanket forfeiture regardless of actual loss may constitute **zulm** (oppression). A Shariah-compliant version would limit forfeiture to actual proven damages.

**Clause 7 — Early Termination** Giving notice and accepting consequences for breaking a contract early is permissible. However **Clause 7.2** — where the landlord forfeits your deposit even when _agreeing_ to let you leave — has elements of **zulm** since you are penalized without the landlord suffering any actual loss.

**Clause 9.1 — Landlord's Absolute Discretion on Renewal** "Absolute discretion" introduces uncertainty about your future housing situation. Not strictly haram but the lack of defined criteria introduces **gharar** into a significant life decision.

**Clause 12 — Indulgence Not a Waiver** Technically fine but could be used oppressively — if a landlord verbally agrees to something and later uses this clause to override it, that would contradict the Islamic principle of **honoring verbal commitments**.

**Clause 16 — Service by WhatsApp PDF** Serving legal documents informally via WhatsApp raises **authenticity and verification** concerns. Islamic contracts emphasize reliable witnessing and documentation (Quran 2:282). Not haram but worth strengthening.

## ❌ Not Shariah Compliant

**Clause 10.4.1(3) — 5% Per Annum Interest** This is the only explicit **riba** clause in the agreement. As discussed previously, this must be removed and replaced with a halal alternative. This is the clearest violation and the one with the strongest scholarly consensus against it.

**Clause 4.1 — Tenant Pays All Landlord Legal Fees Unconditionally** This clause requires you to pay the landlord's legal costs regardless of whether his claim against you is valid or successful. Forcing someone to bear costs for a potentially unjust legal action against them has elements of **zulm**. A Shariah-compliant version would limit fee recovery to cases where the landlord's claim is upheld.

**Clause 18 — Padlocking Without Court Order** This is arguably the most problematic clause from an Islamic perspective. Taking unilateral action to deprive someone of their shelter — even temporarily — without due process mirrors **ghasb** (unlawful seizure of property). Islamic jurisprudence requires disputes to go through proper adjudication before anyone's rights are physically restricted. This clause should be removed entirely.

---


The three absolute priorities from an Islamic perspective are removing **Clause 10.4.1(3)** (riba), **Clause 18** (ghasb), and **Clause 4.1** (zulm) — in that order of scholarly consensus against them.
