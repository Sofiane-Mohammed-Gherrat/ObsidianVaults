### Summary
**Italy is a genuine hidden gem for AI.** Universities in Italy are very affordable compared to their counterparts in Western Europe, and the quality of education is undeniable. The number of fully English-taught AI bachelor programs is small but excellent. [Study.eu](https://www.study.eu/article/belgium-tuition-fees-and-cost-of-living)

**The standout option** is the joint B.Sc. in AI across University of Pavia, University of Milano Statale, and University of Milano-Bicocca. Non-EU students with a valid study visa can benefit from a flat rate set on the basis of their country of citizenship and field of study, ranging from €400 to €4,500 per year. That's potentially the cheapest AI bachelor anywhere in the EU — with three strong research universities behind it. The catch: there are 180 available places for 2025–2026, of which 50 are set aside for non-EU students residing abroad, and non-EU applicants must take the SAT as their admission test.

**Sapienza Rome** is the strongest individual option — with a modest annual tuition of around €3,016 and a small application fee, the program remains accessible while maintaining high academic standards. Sapienza can offer such affordable rates thanks to public funding from the Italian Ministry of Higher Education. [KU Leuven](https://www.kuleuven.be/scholarships/year/2025-2026/faculty-of-engineering-science-fee-waiver)[KU Leuven](https://www.kuleuven.be/scholarships/year/2025-2026/hiw-partial-tuition-fee-waiver-for-non-eea-citizens)

**Bocconi** is expensive at €17,000/yr but worth considering if your academic profile is strong — for students enrolling in their first year in a Bachelor of Science program in 2026–27, tuition at Bocconi is €17,000 per year, but their scholarship program is one of the most generous in Europe and can bring that to near zero. [Università degli Studi di Milano Statale](https://www.unimi.it/en/study/bachelor-and-master-study/fees-and-how-pay-them/fees-2026/2027)

> **One critical note for you:** Italy requires non-EU students to go through a **pre-enrollment process at the Italian Embassy in Kuala Lumpur** before getting a student visa — this has an annual deadline (typically April–May). ### Summary
Here's the honest picture for Belgium as a Malaysian student:

**The good news:** Belgium has some genuinely unique AI programs. VUB's AI program stands out as Belgium's only dedicated bachelor's degree in artificial intelligence, building on their 40-year legacy of AI research and innovation. [Bachelorsportal.com](https://www.bachelorsportal.com/study-options/268550474/artificial-intelligence-czech-republic.html)

**The catch — cost:** Belgium sits in a middle ground. Tuition fees in Belgium are subsidised by the Flemish Government, making them lower than in many other countries for EU students, but as a Malaysian (non-EEA) student, you'll pay the higher rate. A non-EEA student at VUB pays a fixed amount of €1,900 plus €51 per credit — for a standard 60-credit year, that totals €4,960. At KdG, the non-EEA tuition is €7,694/year. For Howest, EEA students pay €1,116 while non-EEA students pay €7,116 per year. [VILNIUS TECH + 3](https://vilniustech.lt/index.php?lang=2)

**Living costs:** As an international student in Belgium you can expect to spend approximately €700–€1,000 per month. Accommodation ranges between €300–€500 per month. Brussels actually has cheaper average rents than London, Amsterdam, Paris, and Berlin. After graduation, non-EU students can apply for a residence permit for an orientation year and stay in Belgium for 12 months to look for a job or start a business. [GoToUniversity](https://www.gotouniversity.com/university/vrije-universiteit-brussel/tuition-fee)[GoToUniversity](https://www.gotouniversity.com/university/vrije-universiteit-brussel/tuition-fee)

> **My recommendation for you:** VUB is your best bet — it's the cheapest for non-EEA students (~€4,960/yr), it has the only dedicated AI bachelor in Belgium, and it's in Brussels where EU institutions, tech companies, and AI startups create excellent internship opportunities. The April 1 application deadline for non-EEA students is coming up relatively quickly, so worth moving on that.

### Summary
**The honest situation:** Romanian university qualifications are highly appreciated and recognised in Europe, with the lowest tuition fees and living costs in Europe — but Romania doesn't yet have a fully dedicated English-taught AI bachelor. What it does have is several excellent public CS programs with strong AI tracks at remarkably low cost. [Study.eu](https://www.study.eu/article/lithuania-tuition-fees-and-cost-of-living)

**The top universities for AI/CS in Romania** are well-documented. The Technical University of Cluj-Napoca, the Alexandru Ioan Cuza University of Iași, and the Academy of Economic Studies in Bucharest produce the best IT professionals in Romania, with UTCN's Faculty of Automation and Computers consistently ranked #1 nationally. UBB has ranked first in the national University Metaranking since 2016 and became the first Romanian university to receive a QS Five Stars rating in 2021. [Karel de Grote Hogeschool](https://www.kdg.be/en/programmes/what-is-the-cost-of-studying)[Vrije Universiteit Brussel](https://www.vub.be/en/studying-vub/practical-info-for-students/how-much-does-studying-cost)

**The government scholarship is a game-changer** — but comes with a condition. The Romanian Government Scholarship covers full tuition fees, university dormitory accommodation, and a monthly stipend for the entire duration of studies, and is open to all citizens of non-EU countries with no restrictive list of nationalities — students from over 121 countries currently study in Romania through this scheme. However, bachelor's and master's degree candidates can only apply for study programs taught in Romanian — the scholarship is not available for English-taught programs. A free Romanian language preparatory year is included, with two semesters of 14 weeks each covering general and technical Romanian vocabulary, and the monthly stipend is maintained throughout. [KU Leuven + 2](https://www.kuleuven.be/english/education/student/fees/tuition-fee-breakdown)

**For the English-taught route** (paying tuition), West University of Timișoara charges €2,430/year for non-EU students in its Computer Science program — the most affordable confirmed English-language CS fee in Romania. Living costs in Romania are approximately €400–700 per month including accommodation (€150–400), food (€150–250), and transportation (€20–40). [KU Leuven](https://www.kuleuven.be/english/education/student/fees)[Vrije Universiteit Brussel](https://www.vub.be/en/studying-vub/all-study-programmes-vub/bachelors-and-masters-programmes-vub/school-thinking/admission-and-enrolment)

**For an Algerian student**, Romania is arguably the most cost-effective EU country overall — especially if you're willing to learn Romanian and apply for the government scholarship (next deadline: March 2027). Ready for the **final master comparison across all countries** we've explored?
